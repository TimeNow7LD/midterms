Why squares instead of a map?

Every state has exactly one governor, so every state gets the same size square. On a regular map, big, empty states like Montana dominate the picture, while small ones like Rhode Island nearly disappear, even though each counts the same.

How to read it

  • Solid colors are races decided in 2026.
  • Pale colors are the 14 states that didn't elect a governor this year. Kentucky, Louisiana and Mississippi vote in 2027. New Jersey and Virginia vote in 2029. The rest vote in 2028.
  • A white dot marks a state that flipped parties in 2026: Alaska, Georgia, Iowa, Nevada, Ohio and Texas.

Term limits: who can run again?

Tap any state to see which term its governor is serving in 2027 and that state's limit. Rules differ widely: 28 states cap consecutive terms (usually two), 9 cap lifetime service, Virginia bans back-to-back terms, and 13 states have no limit at all. Vermont's Phil Scott, for example, is starting his sixth two-year term.

When do they take office?

Most new governors are sworn in during January 2027, but the exact date varies by state. The new Congress is sworn in on January 3, 2027.

About the $80 estimate: it's our calculation of what one 40-hour-a-week earner needs today for the same life: a brand-new home on a 20-year mortgage, a parent at home raising three kids, and no government benefits. Sources and method

  • Race calls: NBC News projections and MultiState's results tracker, with Florida from The Washington Post, Vermont from WCAX and Kansas from the Kansas Reflector. A state is shown as decided only when a winner has been projected by a news organization or the opponent has conceded.

  • Term-limit rules: Ballotpedia, "States with gubernatorial term limits." Terms served are counted from each governor's start date; partial terms are noted as "full."
